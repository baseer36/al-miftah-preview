How to Learn Tajweed at Home

What you can learn alone, and what you cannot

Tajweed can be studied at home, as long as you are honest about which parts need another person.

!
Why you cannot correct yourself

This is not a matter of effort or intelligence. Your brain hears what you intended, not what you produced. Recording yourself helps a little, but only for errors you already know to listen for.

The order to study in

  1. 1
    Makharij

    Before any rule. The rules describe what happens when letters meet, and they are meaningless if the letters themselves are wrong.

  2. 2
    Noon saakin and tanween

    These occur constantly, so every page you read becomes practice.

  3. 3
    Meem saakin

    Three rules, quickly learned.

  4. 4
    Madd

    Start with natural madd and get the count consistent before touching the derived types.

  5. 5
    Qalqalah, ghunnah and the heavy letters

    The finishing layer.

For the rules themselves, see the Tajweed rules explained; for the first step, the seventeen articulation points.

How to practise at home

How to tell if you are improving

Not by how it sounds to you; it will sound the same either way.

Record yourself reciting the same short passage once a month, and have someone qualified listen to the recordings in order. Real improvement shows up as errors disappearing, and you will usually be surprised by which ones were still there.

Signs of real improvement

  • A listener points out fewer errors each month
  • You can name the rule before you reach the word
  • Your madd counts are the same on every page

Signs you are standing still

  • You feel more confident but the same errors are heard
  • You can explain rules but cannot apply them while reading
  • You only ever practise alone

How long it takes

Six to twelve months for the complete set of rules, studied steadily. If you would rather not do it alone, the Tajweed course pairs the rules with a tutor who listens to every lesson.

Questions

Common questions

Can I learn Tajweed from YouTube?

You can learn the rules from YouTube. You cannot have your pronunciation corrected by it, and that is the part that actually requires teaching.

Do I need to finish learning to read before starting Tajweed?

You need to read reasonably fluently. Tajweed applied to halting reading just makes both harder.

How often should I practise?

Short and daily beats long and weekly. Fifteen minutes of reading aloud every day will do more than two hours once a week.

What is the best order to learn the rules?

Makharij, then noon saakin and tanween, then meem saakin, then madd, then qalqalah, ghunnah and the heavy letters.

How do I know I am pronouncing a letter correctly?

You usually cannot, by yourself. A qualified listener can tell within moments, which is why recordings reviewed by a teacher are the best self-check.

Is it worth learning Tajweed as an adult?

Yes. Adults often progress faster on the rules and slower on pronunciation, and correct recitation is something you keep for life.

Should children learn Tajweed too?

Yes, from the start, gradually. A child who learns correct pronunciation early never has to unlearn a wrong habit.
