How to Read the Quran as a Beginner

Starting as an adult is not harder than starting as a child. It is different. This guide sets out the order that works and the shortcuts that do not.

The order that works

First the letters and their sounds. Then the letters joined. Then the vowel marks. Then short words. Then verses. Then pages.

  1. 1
    Letters and sounds

    Each letter from the correct place in the mouth.

  2. 2
    Letters joined

    The same letters in their start, middle and end shapes.

  3. 3
    Vowel marks

    Short vowels, sukoon, shaddah, tanween, madd.

  4. 4
    Short words

    Reading what you have never seen, slowly and correctly.

  5. 5
    Verses, then pages

    Accuracy first, fluency after.

That order is not negotiable, and every attempt to skip a stage shows up two stages later as a problem that takes longer to fix than the stage would have taken to do.

What adults get wrong, and what they get right

What adults get right

  • They can study between lessons, which children largely cannot
  • They understand why a rule exists, so they can self-correct everything except pronunciation
  • Twenty minutes a day of independent practice roughly halves the time to fluent reading

What adults get wrong

  • Memorising instead of reading. Learning the sound of a familiar surah and believing you are reading it
  • Learning from transliteration. English letters cannot represent several Arabic sounds, so it teaches a wrong pronunciation
  • Chasing speed. Fluency is a by-product of accuracy; pushed for its own sake it locks in errors faster
✓
A simple test

Open a page you have never recited and read it. If you can decode it slowly and correctly, you are reading. If you stall completely, you have been memorising.

A realistic timeline

Three to six months on the Noorani Qaida to read accurately. Then one to two years of Nazra to read the whole Quran fluently, depending on days per week. Anyone promising substantially faster than that is describing memorised recitation, not reading.

How fast could you finish the whole Quran?

Once reading is fluent, finishing the Quran is simple arithmetic: 604 pages divided by the pages you read a day.

How to practise between lessons

Read aloud. Always aloud. Reading Arabic silently builds no pronunciation at all, and pronunciation is most of what you are learning.

Should you learn Arabic at the same time?

Reading first, then Arabic. Trying to do both at once usually slows down the reading without speeding up the Arabic. Once you read accurately, Arabic language and Tafseer make every page richer.

Questions

Common questions

Am I too old to learn to read the Quran?

No. Adults regularly complete the Noorani Qaida and move on to fluent reading. Adults tend to progress faster on structure and slower on pronunciation.

How long before I can read a page by myself?

Most adults can decode an unfamiliar page slowly within three to six months, and read it comfortably within a year or two.

Should I learn Arabic at the same time?

Reading first, then Arabic. Trying to do both at once usually slows down the reading without speeding up the Arabic.

Can I learn from transliteration?

It is not recommended. Several Arabic sounds have no English letter, so transliteration teaches a wrong pronunciation that later has to be unlearned.

How many pages is the Quran?

604 pages in the standard Madani mushaf, divided into 30 equal parts called juz’.

How much should I practise each day?

Twenty minutes of reading aloud, every day, does more than a long session once a week.

What if I can recite some surahs from memory already?

That is a good start, but memorised sounds can hide reading gaps. Ask your tutor to test you on a page you have never recited.
