A Realistic Plan for Memorising the Quran

Memorising the Quran is not primarily a feat of memory. It is a feat of revision. This guide explains the method and gives an honest account of how long it takes.

Hifz by the numbers

Memorising the Quran is a long project, and honest planning starts with its size. Figures are for the standard 604-page Madani mushaf, which has 15 lines to a page.

The three daily portions

Why manzil is the whole thing

Almost all abandoned Hifz is abandoned at the same point: when the volume of old material grows large enough that revising it takes longer than learning something new, and the student quietly stops.

From the day revision overtakes learning, memorisation goes backwards. The portion being learned is new, but the portions behind it are decaying faster than they are being replaced.

✓
Why an honest teacher sets a smaller sabaq

The limit is not how much can be learned in a day. It is how much can be revised every day for years.

How long it takes

There is no single honest answer, and anyone who gives one without asking about your schedule is guessing. The arithmetic below shows the shape of it, not a promise.

The variables that matter: how many hours a day, how fluent your reading already is, your age, and, above all, whether revision is genuinely done daily. What a trial class can do is set a pace you will still be keeping in a year, which is worth more than a fast start.

Are you ready to start?

Fluent, correct reading

Memorising on top of weak reading builds errors into the memory.

Time every day

Revision is daily. A gap of a few days costs more than it seems.

A teacher who listens

Hifz has always been taught one to one, by listening.

A pace you can keep

A smaller portion kept for years beats a large one dropped in months.

Memorise with Tajweed from the first attempt

A mistake that is read can be corrected in a moment. A mistake that is memorised is fixed in place and has to be overwritten, which takes many times longer.

This is why fluent, correct reading is required before Hifz begins: not as a formality, but because memorising on top of weak reading builds errors into the memory permanently. See the Tajweed rules for what correct recitation involves.

Reciting from memory is a separate skill

Remembering a passage and reciting it aloud under listening are different. Students who only ever revise silently often find they cannot produce the same passage when someone is listening.

Reciting to a teacher daily is not a test. It is the training.

Questions

Common questions

How long does it take to memorise the Quran?

It depends on hours per day, existing fluency, age and consistency of revision. The realistic pace is set in the trial class, after the tutor hears the student read.

Can Hifz be done online?

Yes. Hifz has always been taught one to one by listening, and a tutor hears sabaq, sabqi and manzil online exactly as they would in person.

What if I forget portions I memorised years ago?

That is normal and it is what manzil exists to prevent. Lost portions are recovered far faster than they were first learned.

What is sabaq, sabqi and manzil?

Sabaq is the new lesson, sabqi is recent revision and manzil is old revision. All three happen every day.

How old should a child be to start Hifz?

Around seven, once reading is fluent. The age matters less than fluent, correct reading and the ability to revise every day.

Can an adult memorise the Quran?

Yes. Adults often learn more slowly per day but bring discipline and understanding. Daily revision is the deciding factor at every age.

How many lines should a student learn each day?

It depends on the student. A teacher sets it after hearing them read, and prefers a smaller portion kept for years to a large one dropped in months.
