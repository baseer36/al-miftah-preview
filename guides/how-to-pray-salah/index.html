How to Pray Salah, Step by Step

This guide covers the prayer from beginning to end — what to do, what to say, and in what order. It assumes you are starting from nothing.

Salah at a glance

The prayer has a fixed shape. Once you see the shape, learning it is a matter of repetition, not of memorising a hundred separate rules.

A note on schools of fiqh

The broad shape of the prayer is the same for every Muslim. Small details, such as hand placement or what is recited behind an imam, differ slightly between schools. Learn the details from a teacher who follows your family’s school.

Before you begin: wudu

Wash the hands to the wrists, rinse the mouth, rinse the nose, wash the face, wash each arm to the elbow, wipe over the head, wipe the ears, and wash each foot to the ankle. Right side before left throughout.

  1. 1
    Hands

    Wash both hands to the wrists.

  2. 2
    Mouth and nose

    Rinse the mouth, then the nose.

  3. 3
    Face

    Wash the whole face.

  4. 4
    Arms

    Wash each arm to the elbow, right first.

  5. 5
    Head and ears

    Wipe over the head, then the ears.

  6. 6
    Feet

    Wash each foot to the ankle, right first.

The five prayers

The positions, in order

  1. 1
    Qiyam: standing

    Facing the qiblah, hands raised for the opening takbir and then placed on the body.

  2. 2
    Ruku: bowing

    Back flat, hands on the knees.

  3. 3
    Qawmah: rising

    Standing again, upright and still.

  4. 4
    Sujood: prostration

    Forehead, nose, both palms, both knees and the toes of both feet on the ground.

  5. 5
    Jalsa: sitting

    A brief pause between the two prostrations. Then a second sujood completes one rak’ah.

When you make a mistake

If you forget whether you have prayed three rak’ahs or four, build on what you are certain of, complete the prayer, and perform two prostrations of forgetfulness (sujood as-sahw) before or after the final salutations.

If you miss a prayer entirely, pray it as soon as you remember. A missed prayer is made up, not abandoned.

✓
If you cannot stand

You may pray sitting, and if that is not possible, lying down. The prayer is not dropped because of illness or injury.

The most common mistakes

Learning it properly

Almost everyone who learns salah from a page carries at least one error: most often in the pronunciation of Al-Fatihah, sometimes in the position of the hands or the stillness between positions.

The fastest fix is to perform the prayer once in front of someone qualified to watch. It usually takes a single lesson to find errors that would otherwise last for years.

Questions

Common questions

How long does it take to learn to pray?

Most students can perform all five prayers correctly within six to ten weeks, with the Arabic pronunciation taking the longest.

Can I pray if I do not understand Arabic?

Yes. The prayer is performed in Arabic, and you learn what the words mean alongside learning to say them.

What if I cannot stand?

You may pray sitting, and if that is not possible, lying down. The prayer is not dropped because of illness or injury.

How many rak’ahs are obligatory each day?

Seventeen: two at Fajr, four at Dhuhr, four at Asr, three at Maghrib and four at Isha.

What if I miss a prayer?

Pray it as soon as you remember. A missed prayer is made up, not abandoned.

Do men and women pray differently?

The prayer is the same, with small differences in some details between schools. A teacher who follows your family’s school will show you.

Can a child learn salah online?

Yes. A tutor watches and listens in a live video lesson, which is how the prayer is checked.
