The seventeen points at a glance

Every Arabic letter is produced at a specific point in the mouth or throat. There are seventeen such points, grouped into five regions. Getting them right is the foundation of Tajweed, and the part that most self-taught readers never address.

17articulation pointsAcross five regions.
5regionsEmpty space, throat, tongue, lips, nasal passage.
28letters placedPlus three long vowels from the empty space.
10tongue pointsThe largest and most demanding region.

The throat, from deepest to shallowest

English speakers almost universally struggle with ع and ح, because English has no sound made at that depth. ع is frequently flattened into a vowel and ح into an ordinary h, which changes words.

The tongue: where most errors live

The common English-speaker errors here are predictable: ط collapsed into an ordinary t, ص into an s, ض into a d, and ظ into a z. In each case a heavy letter has been replaced with its light counterpart.

The lips

The pairs that get confused

A wrong makhraj sounds correct to the person producing it. These are the pairs teachers drill back to back, because the difference only becomes obvious when they are heard together.

How to actually fix a makhraj

  1. 1
    Isolate it

    Say the letter with a sukoon after a hamzah, repeatedly, until the position is consistent.

  2. 2
    Contrast it

    Pronounce it directly against the letter it is confused with: ت against ط, س against ص.

  3. 3
    Put it in words

    Only then say words that contain it, slowly.

  4. 4
    Have it heard

    Ask a qualified listener. You cannot assess this yourself.

!
The whole difficulty

This cannot be self-assessed. A wrong makhraj sounds correct to the person producing it.

Questions

Common questions

How many makharij are there?

Seventeen articulation points, grouped into five regions: the empty space, the throat, the tongue, the lips and the nasal passage.

Which Arabic letter is hardest for English speakers?

ض is generally considered the hardest, followed by ع, ح and the heavy letters ط, ص and ظ, which are commonly replaced by their light counterparts.

Do I need makharij if I only want to read, not recite beautifully?

Yes. Several makharij errors change one word into a different word, which is a matter of meaning rather than of beauty.

Can makharij be learned from a book?

The positions can be learned from a book. Producing them correctly cannot, because you cannot hear your own errors.

How long does it take to fix a makhraj?

It depends on the letter and the habit. Months of short daily repetition are typical for the hardest ones, because it is a physical habit.

Is it the same for children and adults?

Children usually pick up new sounds more easily; adults compensate with focus and a clear understanding of where each sound sits.

What comes after makharij?

Sifaat, the qualities of the letters, then the rules for noon and meem saakin, madd and qalqalah.
