Noorani Qaida

Noorani Qaida Online — Letter by Letter

The Noorani Qaida is where almost every student of the Quran begins. It teaches the Arabic letters, their sounds, and how those sounds join — so that when you open the Quran, you are reading it, not guessing at it.

The syllabus

What the course covers, in order

  1. The Arabic letters in isolation

    Each of the 29 letters by name, shape and sound, with the tutor correcting pronunciation live rather than after the fact.

  2. Letters in their joined forms

    How a letter changes shape at the beginning, middle and end of a word, and how to recognise it in each position.

  3. Harakat — the short vowels

    Fathah, kasrah and dammah, and the habit of reading a letter with its vowel as a single sound rather than two.

  4. Tanween, sukoon and shaddah

    The marks that change length, stress and doubling — the first point where careless reading becomes audible.

  5. Maddah and the long vowels

    Holding a sound for its correct count, which is where Tajweed properly begins.

  6. Joining words and reading aloud

    Short words, then phrases, then the opening verses — read aloud every lesson, corrected every lesson.

What the Noorani Qaida is

The Noorani Qaida is a short primer that teaches the Arabic script from nothing: the letters, the sounds, the marks around them, and how they join into words. It is the usual first step for a child or an adult beginner, because it makes everything after it accurate.

The marks you will learn to read

What real progress looks like

Real progress

  • Reads a line never seen before, slowly and correctly
  • Produces the same sound for the same letter every time
  • Corrects themselves when a word does not sound right

Not yet progress

  • Recites familiar pages fluently but stalls on new ones
  • Names the letters quickly but from the wrong place
  • Moves on because the page count says so

Questions

Common questions about this course

What is the Noorani Qaida?

It is a short, structured primer that teaches the Arabic letters and their sounds in a fixed order, so a student learns to read the Quran accurately from the very first letter rather than imitating what they have heard.

How long does the Noorani Qaida take?

Most students finish in three to six months. A child taking two classes a week will usually take longer than an adult taking five; the course ends when the student can read accurately, not on a fixed date.

Can an adult start with the Noorani Qaida?

Yes, and many do. Adults often move through it faster than children because they can study between classes, though they sometimes need more time on pronunciation.

Does my child need to know Arabic first?

No. The course assumes no Arabic at all. That is its purpose.
