The syllabus

What the course covers, in order

  1. Short surahs from Juz Amma

    Starting with what the student already half-knows by ear, so the first lessons build confidence rather than test it.

  2. Reading with the vowels, at speed

    Moving from letter-by-letter to word-by-word, then phrase-by-phrase.

  3. The waqf marks

    Where to stop, where stopping changes the meaning, and where to continue.

  4. Applied Tajweed

    The rules introduced where they occur in the passage being read, rather than memorised as abstract lists.

  5. Reading through the Quran

    A steady pace, page by page, with the tutor tracking exactly where accuracy slips and returning to it.

What Nazra is

Nazra means reading the Quran from the mushaf itself, as opposed to from memory. After the Noorani Qaida teaches the script, Nazra is where a student reads the Quran, with correction in every lesson, until they can read the whole of it fluently.

Questions

Common questions about this course

What does Nazra mean?

Nazra means reading the Quran while looking at the text, as opposed to Hifz, which is reciting from memory. It is the ordinary way most Muslims read.

Do I need to finish the Noorani Qaida first?

Usually yes. If you can already read Arabic letters with their vowels, the trial class will show where to start.

How long does it take to read the whole Quran?

With steady classes, most students complete a full reading in one to two years. The pace depends on days per week and on practice between lessons.
